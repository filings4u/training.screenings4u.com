Learners and employers must verify that all applicable requirements are satisfied before a learner performs regulated or safety-sensitive duties.

6. DOT Specimen Collector Training

Online DOT specimen collector coursework alone does not establish qualification to perform regulated collections.

For the urine specimen collector pathway described on our website, qualification includes applicable training and the required proficiency demonstrations, including five consecutive error-free mock collections monitored by a qualified person.

The requirements for the collection method and role must be evaluated separately. Training in one specimen type does not automatically qualify a learner for another specimen type, alcohol testing, instructor duties, or other specialized functions.

Learners and employers are responsible for confirming that required training, demonstrations, documentation, and any applicable refresher or error-correction training have been completed.

7. Designated Employer Representative Training

Designated Employer Representative (“DER”) training provides education concerning the responsibilities of the role.

Completion does not create a universal federal DER certification, designate the learner as an employer’s DER, or transfer the employer’s responsibilities to screenings4u.

The employer remains responsible for properly designating an authorized representative, establishing an appropriate program, and ensuring that decisions and actions comply with applicable requirements.

8. Supervisor and Reasonable Suspicion Training

Supervisor training addresses recognition, documentation, and response within the scope of the selected course.

It does not authorize a supervisor to diagnose substance use, make medical conclusions, or disregard applicable testing procedures, employee rights, or employer policies.

Reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause determinations must be based on the standards applicable to the employer’s program. Training requirements and procedures may differ among DOT operating administrations and non-DOT workplaces.

Employers must verify that course content, duration, and documentation meet their specific obligations.

9. Employee Education

Employee training provides general instruction about workplace responsibilities, prohibited conduct, testing processes, and related topics within the selected course.

It does not replace an employer’s required policies, notices, educational materials, acknowledgments, or workplace-specific instruction.

Employers remain responsible for communicating the rules that apply to their workforce and addressing any additional agency-specific or jurisdiction-specific requirements.

10. Hazardous Materials Training

Hazardous materials training must match the functions an employee actually performs.

An online course may not address every material, shipping method, packaging operation, security plan, emergency procedure, or transportation mode relevant to a particular workplace.

Employers remain responsible for identifying applicable training, providing any necessary function-specific and workplace-specific instruction, ensuring appropriate testing, and maintaining required records.

Course completion alone should not be interpreted as authorization to perform every hazardous materials function.
